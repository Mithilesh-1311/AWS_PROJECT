# Scientific & Engineering Methodology

## 1. Biophysical Foundations of EOG

Electrooculography (EOG) records the bioelectric potentials generated by the permanent electrical dipole of the human eye. The eye functions as an electrical dipole where the cornea is electrically positive relative to the negatively charged retina (the **corneo-retinal dipole**, typically exhibiting a standing potential of 0.4 to 1.0 mV).

As the eye rotates within the orbit to shift gaze (a saccadic movement), the orientation of this electrical vector shifts relative to surrounding cranial tissues. Electrodes situated on the periorbital skin detect these continuous potential differentials.

Traditional clinical and engineering setups record EOG using only two bipolar channels:
1. **Horizontal EOG (hEOG)**: Electrodes placed at the outer canthi of both eyes.
2. **Vertical EOG (vEOG)**: Electrodes placed supra-orbitally and infra-orbitally around one eye.

While 2-channel EOG suffices for coarse cardinal movements (left, right, up, down), it lacks the spatial sensitivity required to discriminate fine-grained angular trajectories and non-cardinal saccades.

## 2. University of Malta Isotropic 16-Electrode System

The dataset utilized in this project was acquired by researchers at the University of Malta using an **isotropic circular montage of 16 monopolar electrodes** placed circumferentially around both ocular orbits.

### Key Recording Specifications:
- **Electrodes**: 16 active monopolar channels referenced to an earlobe/mastoid ground.
- **Sampling Frequency ($f_s$)**: 256 Hz (sample period $T_s \approx 3.90625$ ms).
- **Hardware Filtering**: Analog bandpass with high-frequency cut-off at 30 Hz and 50 Hz notch filter to attenuate powerline interference.
- **Stimulus Protocol**:
  - Outward saccade (CS=1): Visual cue jumps from centre to target (fixed magnitude of 12° eccentricity at 36 radial angles separated by ~10°).
  - Return saccade (CS=2): Visual cue returns from target to central fixation.
  - Blink interval (CS=3): Dedicated rest period allowing natural blinking and preventing blink artifacts from contaminating saccade windows.

## 3. Early Intent Detection Formulation

### 3.1 The Problem: Latency vs. Accuracy Tradeoff
A standard human saccade covering 12° of visual angle exhibits a duration of approximately 40–60 ms, preceded by an ocular motor reaction time (physiological latency) of roughly 150–250 ms after visual stimulus presentation.

In traditional gaze-tracking interfaces, the classification algorithm waits for the full saccade to conclude and the eye to stabilize at the target fixation before decoding the user's intent. This introduces significant lag (300+ ms), which is detrimental to high-throughput assistive communication, powered wheelchair navigation, and interactive augmented reality.

**Early Intent Detection** aims to classify the intended saccade trajectory within the earliest fractions of the saccadic burst—observing only the first **20 ms to 100 ms** after saccade onset.

```
Visual Cue (CS=1)             Saccade Onset               Saccade Complete
       |                            |                            |
       | <--- Latency: 150-250ms --> | <--- Duration: 40-60ms --> |
       |----------------------------|=============|--------------|
                                    ^             ^
                                    Window: 20ms  Window: 60ms
```

### 3.2 Saccade Onset Detection vs. Cue Onset
Crucially, our pipeline distinguishes between two reference points:
1. **Cue-Aligned**: Window begins at sample $t_0$ when $CS=1$ appears. This includes the subject's variable neural reaction time and baseline noise.
2. **Onset-Aligned**: Window begins at the detected physiological onset $t_{onset}$, determined when the rectified first derivative of the multi-channel spatial norm exceeds a baseline noise threshold (computed via Median Absolute Deviation on the pre-movement quiet interval).

## 4. Digital Signal Processing & Causality Constraints

Real-time brain-computer and human-machine interfaces impose strict causality constraints: an algorithm running in real time cannot use future samples.

### 4.1 Causal Baseline Correction
Each trial is baseline-centered by computing the mean voltage of the first 25 samples (~97.6 ms) of the outward phase and subtracting it across channels:
$$x_{centered}[c, t] = x[c, t] - \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} x[c, k]$$
Because $K$ lies strictly within the pre-saccadic reaction interval ($< 100$ ms), no future saccade information is leaked.

### 4.2 Causal vs. Offline Filtering
- **Offline Filtering (Visualization)**: Zero-phase forward-backward filtering (`scipy.signal.filtfilt`) provides undistorted phase responses for retrospective analysis.
- **Causal Filtering (Real-time Pipeline)**: Direct Form II transposed IIR filter (`scipy.signal.lfilter`) is utilized when filtering drift, ensuring zero look-ahead bias.

### 4.3 Data Leakage Prevention
All normalisation parameters (z-score mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$) are strictly computed on the training fold and applied to validation/test folds.

## 5. Temporal Windowing at 256 Hz

Continuous millisecond durations must be converted into integer sample counts:
$$N_{samples} = \left\lceil \frac{T_{ms} \cdot f_s}{1000} \right\rceil$$

Using the ceiling function guarantees that the model receives at least the requested observation span:

| Requested Latency ($T_{ms}$) | Sample Count ($N$) | Effective Duration ($T_{eff}$) |
|---|---|---|
| **20 ms** | 6 | 23.44 ms |
| **40 ms** | 11 | 42.97 ms |
| **60 ms** | 16 | 62.50 ms |
| **80 ms** | 21 | 82.03 ms |
| **100 ms** | 26 | 101.56 ms |
| **120 ms** | 31 | 121.09 ms |
| **160 ms** | 41 | 160.16 ms |
| **200 ms** | 52 | 203.12 ms |

## 6. Feature Extraction Pipeline

For each extracted window of shape $(16, N_{samples})$, a feature vector is computed across channels:
1. **Voltage Delta**: $V_{end} - V_{start}$ (captures immediate directional displacement).
2. **Statistical Moments**: Mean, variance/standard deviation, minimum, maximum, and peak-to-peak amplitude.
3. **Trajectory Dynamics**: Linear regression slope across the window:
   $$\text{Slope} = \frac{\sum (t - \bar{t})(s[t] - \bar{s})}{\sum (t - \bar{t})^2}$$
4. **Kinematic Derivatives**: Mean first difference, peak acceleration, and sum of squared derivatives (derivative energy).
5. **Integral Energy**: Area under the curve (trapezoidal integration) and total signal energy.
6. **Spatial Dipole Ratios**: Cross-channel gradient metrics identifying the primary axis of maximum voltage excursion.

## 7. Machine Learning Baseline & Validation Strategy

### 7.1 Direction Class Formulations
1. **8-Class Octant Classification** (Primary baseline): Right ($0^\circ$), Up-Right ($45^\circ$), Up ($90^\circ$), Up-Left ($135^\circ$), Left ($180^\circ$), Down-Left ($225^\circ$), Down ($270^\circ$), Down-Right ($315^\circ$).
2. **4-Class Cardinal Classification**: Right, Up, Left, Down.
3. **36-Class Continuous Radial Classification**: High-resolution angle prediction.

### 7.2 Validation Scheme
- **Within-Subject Cross-Validation**: Stratified Group-K-Fold ($K=5$) over trials to prevent adjacent-sample correlation leakage.
- **Cross-Subject Generalization**: Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) across all 14 subjects to establish subject-independent intent transferability.
